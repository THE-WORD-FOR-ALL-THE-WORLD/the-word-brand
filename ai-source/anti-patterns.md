# Anti-patterns an agent falls into

The failures below are the ones automated tools produce most reliably. The Brand Guide's DON'T
cards and the Messaging Guide's banned-word lists are published separately in this file, generated
straight from the guides. Do not restate those here. This section covers what a model gets wrong
that a designer would not.

## Fabrication dressed as competence

- **Inventing a statistic to complete a sentence.** "Over 1,200 people came to Christ" reads
  finished, so a model reaches for it. Numbers come from the official ministry record. When you do
  not have it, write `[n]` and say the field is unverified.
- **Composing a testimony from patterns.** A testimony that follows the four slots perfectly but
  describes no actual person is a fabrication, not a template fill. The slots are a structure for
  a real, consented, named story.
- **Paraphrasing the prophecy to fit a layout.** It is quoted exactly, in full, or it is absent.
- **Inventing a Bible reference that sounds right.** Verify or omit.
- **Filling a bracketed field to make a draft look complete.** The brackets in the origin story are
  there on purpose, pending verification. Leave them.
- **Naming a city, date, or person from context.** If the record does not say it, the copy does
  not say it.

## Generated imagery

- **Producing a photograph of a congregation, a conference, or the field.** Law VI is documentary
  only. A generated image of ministry is a violation even as a placeholder, even labeled as one.
- **Sourcing a stock photo of hands raised in worship.** An empty slot is better than a borrowed
  moment. Say the slot needs real capture and list what to shoot.
- **Upscaling, restyling, or "cleaning up" a field photograph.** Honest color, natural grading.
  The moment carries the drama.
- **Generating a wordmark.** The mark is an artifact, not live text. Use the published files.

## Register collapse

- **Blending the institution and the movement into one voice.** Serif records and bold sans field
  material are two registers. A page that mixes them reads as neither.
- **Making the parent's formality decorative on initiative material.** Sub-brand work is DM Sans
  led. The serif appears only where the parent speaks.
- **Writing an initiative as if it were a separate organization.** One house, named front doors.
  The endorsement line is not optional.
- **Styling EVERY1 as Every1, EVERYONE, or Every One.** It is EVERY1, always, in every context.

## Palette drift

- **Introducing a tint, shade, or neutral gray that is not in the token set.** A ten percent
  lighter Midnight is not Midnight. If a value is needed and does not exist, ask for it to be
  added to the guide rather than improvising it.
- **Using Parchment as a gray.** It is a warm paper ground, and it is never a neutral fill.
- **Treating Flame and Ember as interchangeable.** Flame is the non-text accent. Ember is fire at
  text size. Reaching for Flame on type is the single most common color failure.
- **Reviving a retired color** because it appears in an old file, a deck, or a screenshot. Two are
  retired by name: `#0077AA` cyan and `#5FAD56` green. The green survives in one place only, as a
  form success state, and never in a brand layout. The Brand Guide stopped listing them at v5.0
  because the guide states what the palette is, not what it used to be. They stay recorded here so
  the audit can still catch them.

## Borrowed authority

- **Reaching for governmental iconography.** No seals, no crests, no flags, no eagles, and no device
  that imitates a government office. The formality of this system comes from restraint, dated
  records, and signatures, not from costume. We carry authority. We do not imitate office.
- **Dressing a document as a legal instrument it is not.** A record carries a kicker, a dateline, and
  a signature because those things are true of it. Adding notary marks, reference numbers, or seals
  to make a page feel official is fabrication in visual form.

## Audit theater

- **Reporting compliance without fetching the manifest.** If retrieval failed, the verdict is
  BLOCKED, not SHIP.
- **Marking checks "n/a" to reach a clean score.** Every `n/a` carries a reason.
- **Auditing against remembered rules.** The version line at the top of the report is a claim about
  which standard was applied. Make it true.
- **Passing gates by not looking.** The gates are the checks most likely to be quietly skipped
  because they require reading the actual asset, caption, or number.

## Scope creep on the standards

- **Writing messaging for something on the roadmap.** SOSKA and the EVERY1 app receive no public
  messaging until launch. Books, music publishing, and clothing are outside the guides entirely by
  ruling of August 2026.
- **Inventing a catchphrase.** If a line has to be explained to be catchy, it is dead. The
  load-bearing phrases are already written.
- **Editing a guide silently.** Every change is proposed in writing, approved by Joel and Nathan,
  version bumped, and recorded in the changelog.
