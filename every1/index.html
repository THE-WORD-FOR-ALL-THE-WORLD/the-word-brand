In plain words

The EVERY1 Movement sends the church out.

What it is

  • A movement of ordinary believers who share Jesus where they already live, work, and study. You are the minister, sent by God everywhere you go.
  • It is a lifestyle, not an event. Membership is simple: you have shared Jesus with at least one person recently. That is the movement.

Who may join

  • Every believer. There is no maturity requirement. When you are born again, you qualify.
  • The movement carries special fire for the young and the newly saved: their fire kindles faster and hotter.

The first three steps

  • Join the weekly prayer meeting, praying for the lost.
  • Share Jesus with one person this week.
  • Take the free personal evangelism course, offered through the School of the Local Church.

How to write as EVERY1

Plain, direct, and close to the field. Shorter sentences than the parent uses. Second person, because the reader is the one being sent.

  • Say what happened. A report of one real conversation is stronger than any adjective about it.
  • Write to the person being sent, not about them. "You are the minister," never "believers are ministers."
  • Keep the ask to one action. A page with three asks gets none of them done.
  • Never make sharing Jesus sound like a technique or a funnel. It is a conversation with a person.
  • Never imply a maturity requirement, an age requirement, or a qualification. The movement's whole claim is that there is none.
  • Numbers come from the record or they are not used. No estimates presented as counts.

Words this brand does not use

These are banned across everything THE WORD publishes, EVERY1 included. Theological words are never banned: these are words that manufacture a feeling or sell.

Hype. next-level, next level, game-changer, game-changing, God showed up and showed out, you won't believe, walls of flame emojis, ALL-CAPS excitement, something big is coming, you do not want to miss this, come expecting

Corporate polish. synergy, engage stakeholders, excited to announce, lean in

Churchy insider talk. traveling mercies, hedge of protection, love on, do life together, press in, contend for breakthrough, a God thing, fresh anointing (unexplained), blessed to announce, get plugged in, authentic community

Generic ministry filler. life-changing, powerful experience, step into your destiny, your best life, new season, shift the atmosphere, unlock your potential, make an impact, transform the world, God-sized dream

The participation layer

Assets designed to be given away.

A movement's identity lives on its people's own feeds, or it is not a movement. These assets exist to leave official hands.

  • The 1 is the glyph. The mark leads with the numeral, and the numeral is published on its own: an app icon, a profile badge, a sticker, a shape simple enough to survive at sixty pixels with no endorsement line in frame.
  • Share cards ride along. Every official EVERY1 surface ships with a version a member can post themselves: square and vertical, footage behind the scrim, one line of Parchment type.
  • The loosest rules in the house. Official surfaces follow this guide. What a member does with the badge on their own feed is not audited; it is the movement working.
  • The icon comes first. The app and the activation platform follow the YouVersion model, so the E1 icon carries the whole identity on its own. It was drawn before the app was built, not after, and it is published here already.
The 1 as a mask

Photography cut into the numeral.

This door’s loudest move, and the one thing it does that no other door does. A photograph is cut into the shape of the 1 rather than set beside it, so the mark and the picture are the same object.

  • The shape is the published file. every1-numeral.svg, never a redrawing and never a font character. It is a mask, so it takes no ink and is never recoloured.
  • The photograph still obeys every rule. Documentary capture, consent before the shutter, a caption where it is published. A shape does not exempt a picture.
  • It holds a photograph and nothing else. Never type, never a logo, never another mark. No outline, no shadow, no rotation.
  • One per view. Used twice on a page it stops being a signature and becomes a pattern.
A new country

Drawn, not generated.

A country lockup carries its country’s name as outlined type, so the build cannot compose one. Each is drawn.

  1. They come from the source file. New country lockups are made in the EVERY1 Canva file, canva.link/saa5nitous3vxts, which is where every mark on this page came from.
  2. The bar carries the country’s flag. Its colours are the flag’s, which is the recorded exception to the six-colour palette. USA is the single exception to the exception: its bar is Flame, White and Midnight, because the flag is close enough that a true red and blue reads as a mistake.
  3. A segment must survive its ground. A black or Midnight segment disappears on Midnight. On a dark ground it lifts, and the published files already do this.
  4. It arrives through the drop zone. Put the export in assets/logos/_inbox/, promote it, and it is cut in three inks with its clear space and minimum size recorded. Email brand@theword.world.

Colour

Six, and no others.

Flame is the accent and the numeral. It is never used for text and never as a ground under text: at text size it does not carry enough contrast. Where you need fire at text size, use Ember.

Midnight#0B1A2D Primary ground. Heroes, bands, footers.
Word Blue#023D6F Structural secondary. Logo continuity.
Parchment#F7F3EC Light ground. Official paper, never gray.
Flame#F85842 Non-text accent: BURN moments, display numerals, highlights. Never carries text. ≤10%.
Ember#C13A24 Fire at text size: primary buttons, links, labels, hovers.
White#FFFFFF Cards, reversed text, button labels.

Never

The short list.

Everything else is judgement. These are not.

  1. Never place any logo on Flame (#F85842).
  2. Never redraw, restretch, rotate, or recolour the mark outside the three published inks.
  3. Never add effects: no shadow, glow, bevel, outline, or gradient.
  4. Never set the mark below its published minimum width.
  5. Never rebuild a lockup by typesetting it. Use the published file.

In the app

The movement on a phone.

EVERY1 is a movement, an app, and an activation platform, so the app is a first-class surface of this brand rather than a place the brand gets applied. These are its screens and what each one is for. They are specified rather than pictured on purpose.

A picture of a screen is correct the day it is exported and silently wrong after the next token change, and no build can tell. These specs are published from the same tokens the app is built from, so they move when the brand moves. Flat mockups may be made from these, and they are illustrative and dated: the spec is what is authoritative.

Rules that hold across every screen

  1. One mask per view. The 1 holding a photograph is this door's signature move, and on a poster it appears once. A scrolling list would put six on screen and turn the signature into wallpaper, so a list uses plain images and only a full-width feature uses the mask.
  2. The numeral is the app icon, on Midnight, with no text. It is the form this door is recognised by at size.
  3. Flame is the accent and never carries text. Buttons, links, and labels use Ember, which holds contrast at text size.
  4. Every control needs default, hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, and empty. A screen without its empty state is not specified.
  5. A dark surface is Midnight, never a darkened Parchment.
  6. Loading is a wash placeholder in the shape of the content, never a spinner over a blank screen.
  7. Errors name what is wrong and how to fix it, in the functional colours. Never Flame, never Ember: Ember means the action, and an error is not an action.
  8. Touch targets are 44px minimum, whatever the control is drawn at.
  9. No endorsement line and no parent lockup anywhere in the app, including the splash and the about screen. This door stands on its own.

Check your work

Before it ships.

Anyone can check a page or a file against this standard without asking us. The checker reads the published rules, so it is always checking against what is current.

python3 brand_check.py poster.html

Download brand_check.py. It needs Python 3 and nothing else: no install, no account, no network.

It decides the mechanical half: colours outside the palette, a face that is not one of the three, text in Flame, a removed focus ring, an image with no alternative text. It does not decide whether a photograph is real or whether the words are true. A clean run is not a finished check.
