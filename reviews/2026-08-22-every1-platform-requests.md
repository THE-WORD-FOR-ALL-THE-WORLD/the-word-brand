# Answer to the EVERY1 platform build

**From:** brand@theword.world
**To:** the EVERY1 platform build
**Answers:** `brand-site-defects.md` (D1 to D6) and `brand-site-requirements.md`
**Released in:** brand system **v7.6**, 2026-08-22
**Verify against:** `https://brand.every1movement.com/ai/manifest.json`

## Short version

All six defects are fixed and every request in the requirements document is answered. Four of the
six defects were ours in the way you described. One, D6, was already decided in the brand and the
EVERY1 manifest was not publishing the decision, which is our fault in a more interesting way and
is dealt with below.

Two things are worth saying before the list.

**Your report was right in every measurable particular.** We reproduced D1, D2 and D3 in one
command each. Your contrast table reproduces exactly against our own implementation: Ember on
Midnight 3.25, on Parchment 4.87, on White 5.39; Flame on Midnight 5.39, on Parchment 2.94, on
White 3.25; Word Blue on Midnight 1.58, on Parchment 9.99, on White 11.05. We have not adjusted a
single one of your numbers.

**The root cause of D2 was one condition in our build.** The EVERY1 manifest was generated from the
full logo set while the copier that populates the site filtered to `width == 1600 and "black" not
in file`. The manifest declared 120 files and the site served 40. There is now one list that both
the manifest and the copier read, and a linter check that fails the build when the two disagree.

## The defects

### D1. No 404. Fixed.

Cause: the EVERY1 Pages project had no `404.html`. Cloudflare Pages answers every unmatched path
with `index.html` and a **200** when a project does not ship one. The portal had one and correctly
returned 404 throughout; the second site never got one.

`every1/404.html` is now generated by the same build, and `brand_lint.py` fails if either site root
loses its 404 page. Unknown paths return 404. Asset routes carry real content types.

You were right that this is the one to fix first. It is what made the other three silent.

### D2. 80 of 120 files not served. Fixed, by publishing all 120.

You said either answer was acceptable and that the mismatch was the defect. We published the files
rather than trimming the manifest, because your reason for wanting a 400px PNG in a mobile header
is a good one and the files already existed in the repository. They had simply never been copied.

All 120 declared URLs now resolve to the file they name, with matching byte length and SHA-256.

### D3. `brand_check.py` advertised, not published. Fixed, by publishing it.

It is live at `https://brand.every1movement.com/brand_check.py` and at
`https://brand.theword.world/brand_check.py`, served as `text/x-python` so you can read it before
you run it. It is declared in the manifest under `checker`, with a checksum.

Run outside this repository it fetches the published manifest, so it always measures against the
current standard rather than a copy of it.

**Please do replace your implementation with it.** Your sentence about enforcing the brand "as we
understood it, not as the brand defines it" is the most important sentence in your document, and it
was a fair description of the position we had put you in.

### D4. The app icon contradiction. Resolved: **the E1 icon.**

`marks[e1].use` was right and `app.rules` was wrong. The rule has been rewritten.

**The E1 icon is the app icon and the avatar on every surface:** the mobile launcher, the
installable web app, the favicon, and every social profile. The answer does not differ between the
web PWA and the mobile app. The numeral is not the app icon; it is the display mark and the shape
photography is masked into, which is a different job.

So the icon you already shipped for the Partnership web PWA was the right one, and you can generate
the mobile launcher icons now. You do not have to draw them: the manifest declares them under
`appIcon.files`, cut from the master by our build.

| File | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `every1-icon-512.png`, `-192.png` | App icon, opaque Midnight ground |
| `every1-apple-touch-icon-180.png` | iOS, opaque, the OS rounds the corners |
| `every1-icon-maskable-512.png` | Android adaptive, mark inside the safe zone |
| `every1-icon-mono-512.png` | Android status bar, white silhouette on transparency |
| `every1-favicon-32.png`, `-48.png` | Favicon |
| `every1-e1-reversed.svg` | The vector favicon, which browsers prefer |

**On your 16px note:** you were right that 32px is exactly at the boundary, and we have come down on
the side of the rule. There is no 16px EVERY1 icon, because the E1 icon's published minimum width is
32px and shipping one would break our own fourth constraint. Declare the 32 and the SVG; browsers
downscale from there.

The maskable file is padded so the mark's whole bounding box sits inside Android's inner-66% safe
circle. That is measured on the rendered PNG by the linter, not assumed from the padding constant:
the arithmetic said the first value was fine and it overflowed by eight tenths of a pixel.

### D5. No checksums. Fixed.

Your correction was accurate: v7.5 published none. Every file the manifest names now carries `bytes`
and `sha256`. That is the 120 marks, the 8 app icon forms, and the four resources in the new `files`
block (`tokens.json`, `tokens.dtcg.json`, `brand_check.py`, `brand.css`).

You were right that D1 and D5 compound. Both halves are now closed: the site distinguishes a real
resource from a typo, and you can prove the bytes you received are the bytes we published.

### D6. Ember on Midnight. **This was already ruled. The EVERY1 manifest was not publishing the ruling.**

This is the one we owe you the fullest answer on, because you did the work correctly and still could
not get to the answer.

The parent brand system has carried a dark theme for several versions. `/ai/tokens.json` has always
had a system token reading:

> **Accent on dark** `#F85842`. Flame. Ember is 3.3:1 on Midnight and fails, so a text accent on a
> dark ground is Flame. This is the one place Flame carries text, and §03 records it.

That is your option 2, decided before you asked. The problem is that **`build_every1_ai()` published
only `color` and `typography`**. Everything else in the parent token surface, the dark ramp, the
neutrals, the state colours, the type scale with its line heights, was dropped on the way to your
door. What reached you was Flame's role reading "Never carries text", full stop, and Ember's reading
"fire at text size", with no dark theme anywhere. You read that correctly and concluded there was no
legal option, because in what you were given there was not one.

Three things have changed.

1. **The EVERY1 manifest now publishes the whole token surface**, not a two-key subset: `theme`,
   `neutral`, `system`, `typeScale`, `spacing`, `radius`, `elevation`, `motion`, `breakpoint`,
   `contrast`, `separation`, `outcome`, `progression`. Same values as the parent, written by one
   build, so they cannot disagree.
2. **The absolute phrasing has been narrowed everywhere it appeared**, in the guide's Law V, in
   Flame's role text, and in the EVERY1 app rules. The rule now reads: Flame never carries text on a
   light ground; on Midnight it is the text accent, because Ember is 3.25:1 there and fails.
3. **The reasons are published as data.** `contrast.forbidden` lists each prohibited pair with its
   measured ratio, so your build can cite the rule instead of asserting it.

**Please retire `#EE7A5F`.** You were right to be uncomfortable owning it, and right that a derived
lighter Ember lands close enough to Flame to blur two colours we deliberately separate. Use
`accent-on-dark`, which is Flame, on Midnight and on `surface-on-dark` `#172537`. It measures 5.39
and 4.77.

## The requirements

### The format request: machine-consumable tokens at a stable URL

Done. `https://brand.every1movement.com/ai/tokens.json` and `.../ai/tokens.dtcg.json`, the latter in
W3C Design Tokens format, both `application/json` with `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` and a
`version` field. The portal has published a DTCG document for some time; EVERY1 now does too.

Checksums are on every declared file, as you asked in the same paragraph.

### §1. Dark mode

The system was always dual-theme and was publishing it badly. There is now a `theme` block that
names the pairing directly rather than leaving you to infer it:

| Role | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ground | `parchment` | `midnight` |
| raised surface | `white` | `surface-on-dark` `#172537` |
| text | `ink` | `ink-reversed` |
| text, muted | `ink-muted` | `ink-reversed-muted` |
| text, soft | `ink-soft` | `ink-reversed-soft` |
| accent | `ember` | `accent-on-dark` (Flame) |
| success / error / warning | `#5FAD56` / `#8C1D13` / `#8A5B12` | `#7FC177` / `#F0968A` / `#E0B25C` |
| rule / wash | `rule` / `wash` | `rule-light` / `wash-light` |
| focus ring | `ember` | `accent-on-dark` |

`ink-reversed-faint` was genuinely missing and has been added, so disabled controls now have a dark
value.

**On your request for a derivation rule: we are declining to give one, on purpose.** Every dark value
here was chosen against a measured ratio on Midnight and on `surface-on-dark`, not lightened by a
formula. A published formula would be a licence to generate values nobody measured, which is how
`#EE7A5F` happened. The `theme.derivation` field says exactly this. Read the values.

### §2a. Outcome colours

Six published, one per outcome, each with a light and a dark value.

| Token | Light | Dark | Records |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `outcome-ministered` | `#005F5E` | `#00DDDB` | Ministered To |
| `outcome-salvation` | `#007D4C` | `#00C479` | Salvation |
| `outcome-healing` | `#3B4100` | `#B2BE2C` | Healing |
| `outcome-baptism-water` | `#0070BD` | `#9CC3FF` | Baptism in water |
| `outcome-baptism-spirit` | `#4C3DAF` | `#A98FFF` | Baptism in the Holy Spirit |
| `outcome-raised` | `#860048` | `#FF83B2` | Raised from the dead |

None of these were picked. They were derived against three constraints held simultaneously: 4.5:1 on
both grounds of their theme, at least CIEDE2000 8.0 from each other and from Ember, Flame and Word
Blue, and holding that separation under simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia. What was
achieved:

| | normal | protanopia | deuteranopia | tritanopia |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| light theme | 14.59 | 8.83 | **8.75** | 9.24 |
| dark theme | 16.73 | 11.31 | **8.57** | 9.03 |

Those are minimums across every pair, not averages. `tools/colorkit.py` computes them, they are
published at `separation` in the manifest, and `brand_lint.py` fails the build if a future palette
edit pulls two of them together.

**Your immediate problem is solved and the answer is not a shade of orange.** Salvation is green.
Fire is the institution's own voice and is not lent to a data category: an outcome badge in the
Ember family makes every button look like a salvation, which is the collision you reported from the
other direction.

**Two things we want to be honest about.**

First, **colour must not be your only encoding.** Six categories cannot be reliably told apart by
hue alone by every reader at any separation. That is a property of dichromatic vision, not of this
palette. The brand now requires that every outcome carries its own glyph and its written name.
Please treat the colour as the fast channel, not the only one.

Second, **the light values are dark and look heavier than a mockup would suggest.** That is forced,
not stylistic: a colour legible as text on Parchment has to be dark. It is the same constraint that
made your `#E8590C` collide with Ember. A bright gold and a bright orange are not available on a
light ground at text size, whatever a design tool shows.

Two badge treatments are published, both measured: quiet (a White card or the dark panel, a hairline
in the outcome colour, the label in the outcome colour) and solid (the outcome colour as fill, White
label on light, Midnight label on dark). All four combinations clear 4.5:1 for all six outcomes.

### §2b. The progression ramp

Yes, a warming ramp toward Flame is on brand. It is the brand's own metaphor and we are glad you
asked rather than assuming. Nine steps, both themes, ending on Flame itself.

| | 1 Match | 2 Kindling | 3 Twigs | 4 Sticks | 5 Logs | 6 Trees | 7 Forests | 8 Mountain Ranges | 9 The World |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| light | `#F8E4D7` | `#FAD5BF` | `#FBC6A9` | `#FBB694` | `#FBA680` | `#FB956E` | `#FA825E` | `#F96E4F` | `#F85842` |
| dark | `#5C412F` | `#6D462F` | `#7F4B2F` | `#924F30` | `#A55231` | `#B95534` | `#CD5737` | `#E2583C` | `#F85842` |

Both ramps are monotone in contrast against their ground and no two adjacent steps are closer than
CIEDE2000 4.2.

**Three rules come with it, and the first one matters most to you.** The ramp lives inside the fire
band by design, so its upper steps run close to Ember and its last step is Flame. **A ramp swatch may
never be an interactive control**, or a reader cannot tell a stage from an action. Every ramp fill
carries a hairline, because the first steps are deliberately a whisper against their ground. And
step 9 counts against the Flame budget, because it is Flame.

### §2c. State colours

Mostly already published and now reaching you: `success-state`, `error-state`, `warning-state` and
their `-on-dark` counterparts, plus `disabled-control`. The gap was `ink-reversed-faint` for disabled
on a dark ground, which has been added.

### §2d and §3. The text ramp and accessibility as data

`contrast` in the manifest publishes **61 permitted pairs**, each with its measured ratio, the floor
it has to clear, and pass flags for AA, AA-large and AAA. Translucent tokens are flattened over the
ground named in the pair before measuring, because that is the colour a reader actually sees.

**Ember on Parchment is 4.87 and Ember on White is 5.39.** Both clear AA at body size, as you asked
us to confirm.

`contrast.forbidden` publishes **6 prohibited pairs with their measured ratios**, which is your
request to publish the reason rather than only the rule:

| Pair | Ratio | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flame on Parchment | 2.94 | what the rule prevents |
| Flame on White | 3.25 | what the rule prevents |
| Ember on Midnight | 3.25 | use `accent-on-dark` |
| Ember on `surface-on-dark` | 2.87 | use `accent-on-dark` |
| Word Blue on Midnight | 1.58 | it disappears |
| Any text on a Flame fill | 3.25 | Flame is never a ground under text |

`brand_lint.py` fails the build if a published ratio goes stale, and warns if a pair we forbid ever
starts passing, because at that point the rule needs rewriting rather than keeping.

### §4. Type as a scale

The eleven-step scale already carried size, line height and family. It now also carries **weight,
letter-spacing, permitted italic, and figure style per step**, joined into one object so you read one
thing rather than two tables.

- **Tabular numerals**: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on `label`, `caption`, and all three
  `display` steps. That covers your live counters and the official record. All three families carry
  the feature.
- **Italic per step**: the sans steps allow italic freely. The serif steps are marked `one word`,
  which is the existing rule that a sentence-case serif headline may italicise at most one word. The
  `label` step is never italic.
- **Scripture**: published as its own treatment. DM Serif Text at `title-small` or `title`, regular,
  no quotation marks, reference always following on its own line in the `label` step, hairline rule
  above, never reflowed or paraphrased. You were right that a treatment already existed implicitly in
  the Vision lockup; it is now written down.
- **Self-hosting**: ruled. All three families are OFL, which permits self-hosting and bundling.
  Self-host on the web, bundle in the application. Your low-bandwidth point decided this: the field is
  where this brand is used most and where the network is worst.

### §5. Making the constraints enforceable

**Flame ≤10%.** The denominator is now defined. One rendered view: a printed page, a slide, an email,
or the viewport at first paint, judged screen by screen rather than over a scroll height.
Photography and video are excluded from both sides, so fire inside a photograph does not count. The
numerator is every Flame pixel the layout itself draws, counted at its opacity. Ember is not counted.

**Clear space per mark.** Every mark now publishes `capHeight` and `clearSpaceUnits` alongside the
sentence, in the units of that mark's own SVG viewBox, plus a note on how to scale. For the E1 icon:
cap height 209.21, clear space 73.22, at ratio 0.35. Scale by `renderedWidth / viewBoxWidth`.

### §6. App icon forms

Covered under D4 above. All seven raster forms plus the SVG are published, declared and checksummed.
The OG card is specified too: 1200×630, Midnight, with a stated safe area of the middle 1000×470
because several networks crop this square. Square profile pictures use the published app icon files
rather than a cropped lockup. Splash was already specified in `app.screens`.

### §7. Small things

- **Discovery**: `https://brand.every1movement.com/llms.txt` now names every resource including the
  tokens, the DTCG document, the stylesheet and the checker.
- **Prohibited language as data**: already there and staying. `words.bannedWords` in the manifest,
  grouped by category, each with `words`, `patterns` and `contextual` arrays and the reason the
  category exists. `brand_check.py` enforces the same list, which is another argument for using it
  rather than reimplementing it.
- **Core messaging strings as data**: already there. `words` carries vision, mission, promise, plain
  statement, key phrases, first steps, audiences, voice rules and boilerplate.
- **CORS on `/ai/*`**: already present, and now also on `/brand_check.py` and `/assets/brand.css`.

## What we did not do

**We did not change any mark, and we did not relax any of the five constraints.** You asked us not
to and we agree. The 16px favicon question was decided in favour of the minimum-width rule rather
than against it.

**We did not publish a dark-mode derivation formula.** Reasons above.

**We did not move Ember or Flame.** Your outcome-colour collision is solved by moving the outcome
palette out of the fire band, not by moving the identity palette into a different one.

---

## What would help us

**Tell us when the manifest is wrong.** Twice now the manifest has been the thing that misled you:
once by declaring files that were not there, and once by publishing a subset that made a decided
question look undecided. Both were invisible from our side, because our own pages read the guides
directly and never went through the manifest to find out what the brand says. You are the first
consumer to actually use the published contract as a contract, and both defects surfaced the moment
somebody did.

The new `404.html` and the checksums mean a wrong manifest now fails loudly. The linter checks mean a
manifest that declares something we do not serve cannot reach production. But neither of those would
have caught D6, which was a manifest that was correct and incomplete. If something we publish reads
as contradictory or as missing a case you have hit, that is worth an email even when nothing is
provably broken.

**Q177 can be closed.** The answer is `accent-on-dark`, which is Flame, on Midnight. It is the one
recorded place Flame carries text, and the measurement is published beside the rule.
