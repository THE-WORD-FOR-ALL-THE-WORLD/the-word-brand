Why this exists

A changelog says what changed. A review says whether it is working.

The Brand Guide changelog records every edit to the standard. It cannot tell you whether the standard is serving the people who have to use it, because it only ever looks at itself.

A system review looks from the outside in. It asks what a designer, an engineer, an ad buyer, a printer, and a volunteer each find when they arrive, and it says plainly where they leave empty handed. Each review is kept here permanently, so the next one can be measured against the last rather than starting from a blank page.

How a review works

Five steps, so the next one is comparable to this one.

  1. Name the version under examination. A review of v6.8 stays a review of v6.8 forever, and the guide it examined stays reachable in the archive.
  2. Arrive as each audience in turn. A designer, an engineer, an ad buyer, a printer, a volunteer, and an AI agent. Record what each one finds and what each one has to invent.
  3. Separate what is missing from what is wrong. Additive gaps and structural faults get different fixes and different urgency. Say which is which.
  4. Give every recommendation an acceptance test. A recommendation nobody can prove complete is a recommendation that stays open forever.
  5. Record the version it produced. The review closes when the version it called for is published, and the row above says so.

Reviews are expected roughly twice a year, and whenever a new kind of surface joins the system: a new initiative, a new channel, a new platform the brand has to live on.
